Night-time Radiance Analysis of Agra

Annual mean VIIRS night-time radiance and long-term spatial trends from 2014 to 2024.

Study area: Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India

Overview Trend Maps Annual data Methodology

Project overview

This project examines the temporal variation of satellite-observed night-time radiance across the Agra ward-based study area between 2014 and 2024. Monthly VIIRS observations are aggregated into annual mean surfaces to visualise changes over time.

Data and methodology

Satellite data: NOAA VIIRS Day/Night Band monthly radiance collection, available in Google Earth Engine as NOAA/VIIRS/DNB/MONTHLY_V1/VCMSLCFG, using the avg_rad band.

Processing: Monthly observations were grouped by calendar year and averaged to generate annual mean radiance surfaces for 2014–2024. The annual images were clipped to the ward-based Agra study area. Spatial mean radiance was calculated for each year, and the 2024 surface was compared with the 2014 surface.

Results and discussion

Mean radiance declined from 24.7379 nW/cm²/sr in 2014 to a minimum of 20.6445 nW/cm²/sr in 2019. It then fluctuated and increased during 2023–2024. The 2024 mean was 24.4505 nW/cm²/sr, approximately 1.16% below the 2014 value.

The change map indicates spatially varied increases and decreases in observed night-time radiance. These patterns are not uniform across the study area. Radiance alone cannot identify the cause of a change or serve as a direct measure of population or economic activity.

Limitations

The study uses the available ward-boundary dataset as its spatial extent; it should not be interpreted as an independently verified municipal boundary. VIIRS radiance is a satellite-derived indicator affected by spatial resolution, lighting conditions, and data quality. The findings describe observed radiance patterns and do not establish causation.

Data source

NOAA VIIRS DNB Monthly Radiance dataset hosted on Google Earth Engine.

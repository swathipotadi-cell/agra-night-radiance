01 / Project overview

How did observed night-time radiance change?

This project examines temporal variation in satellite-observed night-time radiance across the available Agra ward-based study area. Monthly VIIRS observations are aggregated into annual mean surfaces to visualise spatial patterns and changes between 2014 and 2024.

Headline result: the annual spatial mean decreased from 24.7379 nW/cm²/sr in 2014 to 24.4505 nW/cm²/sr in 2024. The series reached its lowest annual mean in 2019 before rising again.

05 / Workflow

Data and methodology

01 · Data source

NOAA VIIRS Day/Night Band monthly radiance collection in Google Earth Engine.

02 · Variable

avg_rad band from NOAA/VIIRS/DNB/MONTHLY_V1/VCMSLCFG.

03 · Temporal aggregation

Monthly observations grouped by calendar year and averaged to create annual mean surfaces for 2014–2024.

04 · Study boundary

Annual images clipped to the available Agra ward-based study geometry.

05 · Summary statistics

Spatial mean radiance calculated for each year and percentage change computed relative to 2014.

06 · Spatial comparison

Annual surfaces and pixel-wise trends visualised to assess spatial and temporal variation.

06 / Interpretation

Results, discussion & limitations

Key observations

  • Mean radiance declined from 24.7379 nW/cm²/sr in 2014 to a series minimum of 20.6445 nW/cm²/sr in 2019.
  • After 2019, the annual mean fluctuated and increased during 2023–2024.
  • The 2024 mean was 24.4505 nW/cm²/sr, approximately 1.16% below the 2014 value.
  • Pixel-wise patterns indicate that radiance trends may vary spatially within the study area.

Interpretation cautions

Satellite-observed radiance is an indicator of emitted and reflected night-time light, not a direct measurement of population, economic activity, or electricity consumption. Changes in radiance alone do not establish the cause of observed patterns.

Limitations

  • The available ward-boundary dataset defines the study extent; it has not been independently verified here as the official Agra municipal boundary.
  • VIIRS observations are affected by spatial resolution, lighting conditions, atmospheric and viewing effects, and data quality.
  • Annual averaging smooths within-year variation and may conceal seasonal or short-term changes.
